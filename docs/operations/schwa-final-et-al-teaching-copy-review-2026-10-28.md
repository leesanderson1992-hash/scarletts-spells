## `D4_SCHWA_FINAL_ET`

### Page: The disappearing vowel

Today we are looking at why unstressed vowels can seem to disappear in multisyllabic words.

- They are usually said more strongly and with more energy.
- They are usually lighter and less energetic, so their spelling can be hard to hear.

The schwa /ə/ is sometimes called the ‘murmur vowel’ or the ‘lazy sound’. It is one kind of unstressed vowel. A, E, I, O and U can all spell it.

Schwa examples: desperate · des-per-ate, dictionary · dic-tion-ar-y

Other weak-vowel examples: imagine · /ɪ/ at the start, village · /ɪ/ in the last syllable, A weak vowel can sound closer to ‘ah’ in some accents; follow the recording.

A E I O U — and sometimes Y — can be vowel letters.

### Page: The Secret of the Quiet -et

*When the ending looks like -et but sounds like “it”!*

Remember how vowels can become weaker when they are not in the strongest syllable of a word?

Sometimes a word ends with **-et**, but when we say it naturally, the ending sounds more like “it”.

Look at these words. Say them aloud and compare how they sound with how they are spelt.

| Word | Say it like this | Remember the spelling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| basket | BAS-kit | bask-et |
| jacket | JAK-it | jack-et |
| market | MAR-kit | mark-et |
| rocket | ROK-it | rock-et |
| ticket | TIK-it | tick-et |
| target | TAR-git | targ-et |

#### What's the secret?

Your ears might tell you to write **-it**, but these words are actually spelt with **-et**!

The **e** has a weak vowel sound. It doesn't sound like the clear short **e** in **bed**.

#### Detective Tip 1 — Don't let the sound trick you!

- BAS-kit → basket, not baskit
- JAK-it → jacket, not jackit
- MAR-kit → market, not markit

**Detective trick:** The ending might sound like “it”, but it can be hiding an **e**!

#### Detective Tip 2 — Watch out for real -it endings!

Not every ending that sounds like “it” is spelt **-et**.

| Sounds like “it” | Correct ending |
| --- | --- |
| rabbit | -it |
| habit | -it |
| basket | -et |
| jacket | -et |

You cannot always work out the spelling just by listening. You must learn which ending belongs to each word.

### Page: The Disguised E!

*The E is pretending to be I!*

Sometimes, the letter **E** puts on an **I** disguise!

When we say words like **basket**, **jacket** and **rocket**, the ending sounds like “it”.

But underneath that sound, there's a hidden **E** in the spelling.

Picture this: A cheeky letter **E** wearing an **I** costume, with the real **E** peeking out underneath.

#### Remember the -ET team!

| Word | Sounds like | Spelt with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| basket | BAS-kit | ET |
| jacket | JAK-it | ET |
| rocket | ROK-it | ET |
| market | MAR-kit | ET |
| ticket | TIK-it | ET |
| target | TAR-git | ET |

#### Today's Secret Rule

Some words sound like they end in “it”, but they are spelt with **-et**.

The letter **E** is only pretending to sound like **I**!

#### Memory Motto

“Sounds like IT? Don't forget, E might be hiding in ET!”

### Page: The Secret of the Lazy -al

*When the ending -al sounds like ‘uhl’!*

You already know that vowels can become weak when they do not receive the strong beat in a word.

Some words hide a quiet **a** before **l** in **-al**. In the pronunciations shown here, it makes the weak **schwa /ə/** sound followed by **l**.

Listen to these words in British English. The exact syllables can vary by speaker; follow the chosen recording in each activity.

| Written stem | Ending | Spoken cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| natur | **al** | NACH-uh-ruhl |
| actu | **al** | AK-choo-uhl |
| usu | **al** | YOO-zhoo-uhl |

Notice the **a** near the end. It does not make its strong letter sound; in these examples it sounds like /ə/ before **l**. In today's activity words, follow the highlighted reviewed **a** and its recording.

#### Detective Tip 1: Find the hidden A!

Look at **n a t u r a l**. In the clear three-syllable pronunciation, the last **a** has a weak /ə/ sound. In quicker speech, that ending may be even harder to hear.

- natur**al** → NACH-uh-ruhl
- actu**al** → AK-choo-uhl

**Detective trick:** A quiet ‘uhl’ ending has several possible spellings. In these words, remember **-al**.

#### Detective Tip 2: Look for the -ual pattern!

These words share the written **u-a-l** pattern:

- act**ual**
- us**ual**
- fact**ual**
- rit**ual**

The **a** before **l** is weak, but it still belongs in the spelling. The **u** is another vowel: listen to the chosen word recording to hear how it is said.

**Memory trick:** Picture the three-letter spelling pattern **U-A-L**.

#### Detective Tip 3: Ask the word's relatives!

Related words often keep the written **-al** or **-ual** pattern:

- natural → unnatural
- actual → actually
- usual → usually

**Detective trick:** If you know **actual**, its spelling helps you remember **actually**. A relative can preserve a spelling even when it does not make the vowel clearer.

#### Detective Tip 4: Keep the A when adding -ly!

Add **-ly** without dropping the **a** from **-al**:

- natural + ly → naturally
- actual + ly → actually
- usual + ly → usually

Some speakers compress these words in everyday speech. Use the reviewed recording when counting syllables in an activity; use your spelling voice to remember all the letters.

#### Detective Tip 5: Beware of the ‘uhl’ spelling trap!

The endings of **natural**, **camel** and **little** may sound similar, but they are written differently:

- natur**al** → -al
- cam**el** → -el
- litt**le** → -le

Your ears alone may not tell you which ending to choose. Look for **word families, familiar patterns and meaning**.
