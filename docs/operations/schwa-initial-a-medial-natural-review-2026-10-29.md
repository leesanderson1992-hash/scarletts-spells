# Initial A and medial natural: lesson review

This is the editorial gate for the new initial A specialist route and for
`natural` as a *medial* weak-vowel example. Existing approved dictionary
definitions and sentences are reused. The new skill targets, sound choices,
cuts and teaching pages below still require approval before activation.

## Initial A teaching pages

**Page 1 — The disappearing vowel.** The already approved shared Schwa route
introduction is reused without changes. It explains that unstressed vowels may
be weak and that schwa /ə/ is one kind of weak vowel.

**Page 2 — The Quiet First A.** “The a is written even when it sounds weak.”
Some familiar words begin with a quiet **a**. Another syllable gets the strong
beat. Listen to the complete word, then compare its sound with the first written
letter: **a**bout (uh-BOUT), **a**round (uh-ROUND), **a**way (uh-WAY), and
**a**gain (uh-GEN). The quiet first vowel is written **a** in these words.
Pronunciation can vary, so the selected audio governs the spoken cuts and
sound label.

Detective Tip 1: Find the strong beat. Say the four words; the first syllable is
quieter. Keep the first written **a** even when the vowel is hard to hear.

Detective Tip 2: Check the spelling. A quiet first vowel does not tell you its
letter; other words begin with quiet **e** or **o**. Listen to the chosen word audio
and remember the written beginning of the whole word.

**Page 3 — Today's Secret Rule.** In some words, the first written **a** is
unstressed and sounds weak. Keep the **a** when you spell the whole word. Find
the strong beat; look at the first letter; say, look, cover and spell the whole
word. Memory motto: “Hear the quiet start, remember the A!”

The exact source is `lib/adle/weak-vowel/lesson.ts` in this repository.
